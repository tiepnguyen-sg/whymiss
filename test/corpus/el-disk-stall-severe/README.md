# el-disk-stall-severe

The Lighthouse-paired execution client's disk write throughput is throttled much harder than el-disk-stall — 64KB/s instead of 1MB/s — to see whether a more severe saturation pushes the outcome from degraded toward missed.


## What was broken

Fault: cgroup_io applied to el-1-geth-lighthouse for 20s, around slot 291.

## Recorded outcome

- Block observed for the duty slot: true, ~26.9s into the slot.
- Attestation published (seen in the pool): true, effectively simultaneous
  with block_seen.
- Attestation included: true, inclusion delay 1 (the best possible).

## The hypothesis this scenario was testing did not hold up

The 64KB/s throttle here is 16x harder than `el-disk-stall`'s 1MB/s, on the
premise that more severe saturation would push the outcome further from
healthy. It did not: the recorded delay (~26.9s into the slot) is close to
`el-disk-stall`'s (~31s) and `el-disk-stall-prysm`'s (~24.6s), and all three
still ended in the best possible inclusion delay. Worth stating plainly rather
than quietly matching the original hypothesis to the data: this poll-based
measurement (see `Observer`'s doc comment in `tools/faultinjector/observe.go`)
does not appear sensitive enough to discriminate throttle severity — the
~25-31s figure looks more like a property of how this tool measures than of
how hard the disk was actually throttled. The fault was real and the delay is
real; the causal story of *how much* delay the severity difference caused is
not established by this data.
