# network-late-block-r05

The third node's consensus client is paused from eight seconds before its own proposal slot until five seconds into it, so the block is produced late rather than not at all. The watched attester is on node 1 and the independent baseline is node 2; neither is touched, and neither is the proposer.
That three-way split is what makes the scenario possible at all. R-110 requires the watched node and the baseline node to *agree* that the block arrived late, and a node records no gossip arrival for a block it produced itself — so on a two-node devnet the proposer is always one of the two observers and one of the two measurements is always missing. network.late_block was unreproducible by construction until the devnet gained a third node.
Expect the block to arrive past the +4s attestation deadline at both observers, within thresholds.network_deviation of each other, which is R-110's definition of lateness that is the network's and not the operator's.
MEASURED BEHAVIOUR, 2026-08-26. A 13s pause releases the proposer at +5s and the block lands at +5.777s, measured at node 1, with node 2's independent baseline at +5.747s — 30ms apart, both past the +4s deadline, which is exactly R-110's discriminating condition. The block is then **orphaned**: slot 47 returns 404 on the canonical chain and slot 46 stays head, so the watched attester's vote for slot 46 is head-correct and the duty earns every reward flag. whymiss reports network.late_block for a duty that lost nothing, which is correct and is the point — it explains why the slot is empty and why the attestation voted where it did.
Late-but-canonical is not reliably reachable on this devnet, and the arithmetic says why rather than a guess: a block appears roughly 0.8s after release (13s pause -> release +5s -> block +5.777s), all 96 validators across three nodes vote promptly at the +4s deadline, and a block arriving after that has lost proposer boost. The release window that would produce a late block that still wins fork choice is about +3.2s to +3.7s — under half a second wide, and either side of it yields an on-time block (no R-110) or an orphaned one (this record). The orphaned shape is the reproducible one and is what this scenario records.
